Notion is already where a lot of people keep their notes, projects, and databases. Notion AI sits on top of all of that — and the useful bit isn't the writing assistance, it's the automation inside databases. That's where it separates itself from just using ChatGPT in a separate tab.

Here's how to actually use it.

## The Basics: Triggering Notion AI

Type `/AI` on any Notion page, or press the spacebar on an empty line. That opens the AI prompt menu. From here you can write, edit, summarise, brainstorm, or translate directly on the page — no copy-pasting to another tool.

For editing existing text, highlight it and a menu appears with quick options: Improve writing, Fix grammar, Make shorter, Make longer, Change tone. These are fast one-click fixes that work well for polishing content you've already drafted.

That's the surface-level stuff. Most people find it, use it for a few drafts, and then don't go much further. Here's where it gets more useful.

## Database Automation: The Feature Most People Miss

This is Notion AI's strongest capability and the one that saves the most time once set up.

Inside any Notion database, you can add an **AI property** — a column that automatically generates content based on other fields in that row. You set the prompt once, and it applies to every new entry automatically.

Practical examples:

- **Content calendar** — AI property set to: *"Write a one-sentence meta description using the Title and Keywords fields."* Every new article gets a draft SEO description without you touching it.
- **Task database** — AI property: *"List three potential blockers for this task based on the Description."* Surfaces risks before you start work.
- **Meeting notes database** — AI property: *"Summarise the key decisions and action items from these notes."* Every meeting gets a structured summary automatically.
- **Client CRM** — AI property: *"Write a one-paragraph summary of recent activity with this client based on the Notes field."* Quick context before a call without reading everything.

The setup takes a few minutes per database. The time saving compounds every week. This is the use case that justifies the cost of Notion AI far more than the writing features do.

## Summarising Long Pages

If you use Notion to store research, meeting notes, or reference documents, this is worth knowing. Place your cursor at the top of any page, type `/AI`, and select **Summarise**. Notion AI reads the entire page and gives you a condensed overview.

For long research pages or detailed project briefs, this is faster than re-reading. You can also prompt it more specifically:

- *"Extract all action items from this page"*
- *"List the key decisions made in this document"*
- *"Summarise the risks mentioned across this page"*

Highlight a specific section first if you want it to focus on part of a document rather than the whole thing.

## Writing and Brainstorming

For first drafts, Notion AI is useful for the same reason any AI writing tool is — it gets you past the blank page. The difference here is that it already has the context of your workspace if you prompt it correctly.

Useful prompts that work well:

- *"Write a project brief based on the goals and timeline listed above"* — it reads what's already on the page
- *"Generate five potential objections a client might raise about this proposal"*
- *"Create an outline for a guide on [topic] aimed at UK small business owners"*
- *"Write three subject line options for an email about [topic] — direct tone, no clickbait"*

The output is a starting point. Edit it. The value is speed, not perfection.

## Templates with Embedded AI Prompts

If you use Notion templates — and you should — you can embed AI prompts directly into them. When you create a new page from that template, the AI prompt is already there and ready to run.

Examples:
- New project template includes an AI block: *"Generate a list of potential risks based on the project description above"*
- Weekly review template includes: *"Summarise my wins and blockers from this week's notes and suggest three priorities for next week"*
- Client onboarding template includes: *"Draft a welcome email for this client based on their name, company, and project type"*

You build the prompt once. Every time you create a new page from that template, the AI block is pre-loaded and runs with one click.

## Notion AI vs Just Using ChatGPT

The honest comparison: ChatGPT and Claude produce better prose and handle more complex reasoning. For pure writing quality, they're stronger.

Notion AI's advantage is context and integration. It lives inside your workspace, reads your existing pages, and automates inside your databases. You don't have to copy content out of Notion, paste it into another tool, copy the response back, and paste it in again. For people who live in Notion, that friction removal matters.

Use Notion AI for anything that benefits from being inside your workspace — database automation, summarising your own documents, drafting content that references other pages. Use ChatGPT or Claude for anything that requires deeper reasoning, better creative output, or tasks unrelated to your Notion content.

## Pricing

Notion AI is an add-on at **$10/month per member** on top of your Notion plan, or included in the Notion AI plan at **$16/month per member** (billed annually). There's no meaningful free tier for AI features — you get a limited number of AI responses on the free Notion plan before hitting a wall.

If you're using Notion seriously for work and the database automation applies to your workflow, it's worth it. If you're only going to use it for occasional writing help, you're better off with a standalone AI tool you're already paying for.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Notion AI worth paying for?
Depends on how you use Notion. If you use databases heavily and will set up AI properties to automate repetitive fields, yes — the time saving is real. If you're only using it for basic note-taking and occasional writing drafts, a standalone AI tool like Claude or ChatGPT does more for a similar price.

### How do I use Notion AI to summarise a page?
Type `/AI` at the top of the page and select Summarise, or type your own prompt like *"Summarise the key points on this page."* You can also highlight a specific section first to limit what it reads. It works on long documents and meeting notes particularly well.

### Can Notion AI read and reference other pages in my workspace?
It can read the content of the page you're currently on. For referencing other pages, you need to either be on that page or copy the content in. It doesn't browse your entire workspace automatically — it works with what's visible on the current page.

### What is a Notion AI property in a database?
An AI property is a database column that automatically generates content using AI based on other fields in the same row. You write the prompt once — for example, *"Summarise this meeting using the Notes field"* — and it runs for every entry in the database. It's the most practically useful Notion AI feature for people managing content, tasks, or CRM entries.

### Is Notion AI better than using ChatGPT?
For writing quality and complex reasoning, ChatGPT and Claude are stronger. Notion AI's advantage is that it lives inside your workspace — it can read your existing pages, automate database fields, and integrate directly into your templates without any copy-pasting. Both have their place; they solve different parts of the problem.

### How do I change the tone of writing in Notion AI?
Highlight the text you want to adjust, click the AI option that appears in the toolbar, and select **Change tone**. You can choose from professional, casual, confident, friendly, or straightforward. You can also write a custom prompt — *"Rewrite this in plain English, direct and concise, for a UK audience"* — for more specific results.

### Can Notion AI translate content into other languages?
Yes. Highlight the text, open the AI menu, and select **Translate**. It supports a wide range of languages. For professional or client-facing translations, treat the output as a first draft and have it reviewed — it's reliable for general content but not a substitute for a professional translator on anything high-stakes.
